# Edward de Bono — separate the modes, or they cancel each other

## The one trait
Parallel thinking instead of adversarial thinking. Everyone wears the same hat
at the same time, so the room stops defending positions and starts covering
ground.

## The question they ask
**"Which mode are we in right now — and is everyone in it?"**

## Method — Six Thinking Hats, in full

Each hat is a mode. **Only one hat is worn at a time, by everyone at once.**
The facilitator names the hat and times it. Typical 2–5 minutes per hat.

| Hat | Mode | What belongs here | What does not |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⬜ **White** | Information | Facts, figures, what is known, what is missing, what we would need | Interpretation, opinion |
| 🟥 **Red** | Feeling | Hunches, gut reactions, likes and dislikes — **stated without justification** | Reasons. Asking "why" breaks the hat |
| ⬛ **Black** | Caution | Risks, flaws, why this fails, what the law or budget forbids | Pessimism for its own sake |
| 🟨 **Yellow** | Value | Benefits, feasibility, best case, why it could work | Fantasy with no mechanism |
| 🟩 **Green** | Creativity | Alternatives, provocations, new options, "what else" | Evaluation of any kind |
| 🟦 **Blue** | Process | Agenda, which hat next, summary, decision | Content — Blue manages, it does not contribute |

**The run:**
1. **Blue** opens — state the question and the sequence.
2. **White** — establish what is actually known before anyone argues.
3. **Red** — 30 seconds each, no justification. Surfaces positions early so they stop leaking into other hats.
4. **Yellow before Black.** This ordering is deliberate: criticism first kills options that were never developed enough to judge.
5. **Black** — now attack it properly.
6. **Green** — repair what Black broke, or generate alternatives.
7. **Blue** closes — summarise and decide.

**Sequences he specified for common cases:**
- Quick idea assessment: Yellow → Black → Red
- Solving a problem: White → Green → Yellow → Black → Blue
- Choosing between options: White → Yellow+Black on each → Red → Blue

## Method — lateral thinking tools

- **Po (provocative operation).** State something deliberately wrong — *"cars have square wheels"* — and use it as a stepping stone rather than a proposal. The value is the movement, not the statement.
- **Random entry.** Take an unrelated word and force a connection. Breaks the pattern the mind has already settled into.
- **Challenge.** Ask "why this way?" of things that work fine. Not because they are wrong, but because being locked in is the default state of a self-organising system.
- **Concept extraction.** Move up from the specific solution to the concept, then back down to a different specific.

## Real decisions
- Introduced Six Hats into large organisations in the 1980s–90s on the argument that meeting time is wasted defending positions; several corporate case reports claim large reductions in meeting length, though the figures come from de Bono's own organisation and are not independently replicated.
- Argued that thinking is a teachable skill and put it into school curricula (CoRT programme), against the prevailing view that it follows from intelligence.

## Quotes
- "You cannot dig a hole in a different place by digging the same hole deeper."
- "Creativity involves breaking out of established patterns in order to look at things in a different way."

## Blind spots
- The empirical base is thin. Six Hats is widely used and weakly studied; the strongest claims come from de Bono's own materials.
- It needs a facilitator with authority to enforce the hat. Without one it collapses into a normal meeting with colour names.
- The Red hat can license unexamined bias by ruling justification out of scope.
- He was dismissive of academic critique in a way that insulated the tools from correction.

## Not this
Not "assign each person a hat." That is the most common misuse and it rebuilds
the adversarial room the method exists to dismantle. **Everyone wears the same
hat at the same time.**
