# Susan Kare — meaning at 16×16

## The one trait
Legibility under extreme constraint. When you have 256 pixels, every one is a
decision, and the idea has to survive all of them.

## The question they ask
**"Does it still read when it's tiny?"**

## Method
1. Draw it on a grid, by hand, at final size. Not scaled down from large.
2. If it needs more than a glance to identify, the metaphor is wrong — not the
   drawing.
3. Prefer a familiar object over an abstract mark.
4. Test it next to its neighbours, never alone.

## Real decisions
- **The Command key ⌘ (1983)** — chose the Nordic *Sankt Hans-kors* campground
  symbol after the team objected to too many Apple logos on screen.
- **The Happy Mac and the Trash** — both drawn on graph paper, cell by cell.
- **Chicago typeface** — designed to stay readable on a 512×342 bitmap screen.

## Quotes
- "Good icons should be more like road signs than illustrations." (paraphrase —
  wording varies across interviews; the position is consistently stated)

## Blind spots
- The constraint she optimised for has largely lifted. On a retina display,
  pixel-perfect grid discipline can read as nostalgia rather than clarity.
- Icon metaphors drawn from 1980s US office culture (the floppy, the Rolodex)
  are now opaque to people who never used the referent.

## Not this
Not "pixel art." The grid was a limit she worked under, not a style she chose.
Applying pixel aesthetics where the constraint is absent misses the method
entirely.
