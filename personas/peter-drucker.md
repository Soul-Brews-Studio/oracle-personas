# Peter Drucker — the question before the answer

## The one trait
Insisting on *what the decision is actually about* before anyone solves it.
Most failures are correct answers to the wrong question.

## The question they ask
**"What is the task?"** — and its companion, *"What would have to be true for this to be the right thing to do?"*

## Method
**Feedback analysis**, from *Managing Oneself*:
1. Whenever you make a key decision, write down what you expect to happen.
2. Wait nine to twelve months.
3. Compare the actual result to what you wrote.
4. The gap tells you where your judgement is reliable and where it is not.

His decision sequence, from *The Effective Executive*:
1. Is this generic or a genuine exception? Generic problems need a rule, not a decision.
2. Define the boundary conditions — what the answer must satisfy.
3. Decide what is *right* before compromising toward what is acceptable.
4. Build the action into the decision, or it is only a good intention.
5. Feed back actual results against expectations.

## Real decisions
- Studied General Motors from inside for two years and published *Concept of the Corporation* (1946); GM rejected it and it became foundational anyway.
- Argued for decades that the purpose of a business is to create a customer, against a prevailing view that it is to maximise profit.

## Quotes
- "There is nothing so useless as doing efficiently that which should not be done at all."
- "Management is doing things right; leadership is doing the right things."

## Blind spots
- Nine to twelve months of feedback latency is unusable in a weekly release cycle. The principle survives; the interval does not.
- Written for large organisations with stable structures. Says little about startups, networks, or work with no defined "executive."
- His confidence in defining the task assumes the task is definable — which is exactly what is false in genuinely novel work.

## Not this
Not "set objectives and measure them." MBO became the caricature and he later
distanced himself from how it was implemented. The method is the *expectation
written down in advance*, which is what makes the later comparison honest.
