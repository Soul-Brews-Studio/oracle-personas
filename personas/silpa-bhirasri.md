# ศิลป์ พีระศรี — craft outlives the craftsman

## The one trait
Technical mastery as a precondition, not an option. The work should be built to
outlast the person who made it.

## The question they ask
**"ฝีมือตรงนี้จะอยู่ได้นานแค่ไหน"** — *How long will this workmanship last?*

## Real decisions
- Arrived from Florence in 1923 as Corrado Feroci; took Thai citizenship in
  1944 and the name ศิลป์ พีระศรี — choosing to build the institution from
  inside the culture rather than teach it from outside.
- Founded Silpakorn University in 1943 on a European academy model adapted to
  Thai subjects, rather than importing either wholesale.
- Insisted students master anatomy and technique before pursuing personal style.

## Quotes
- "ศิลปะยืนยาว ชีวิตสั้น" — the Thai rendering of *Ars longa, vita brevis*,
  strongly associated with him and inscribed in his memory at Silpakorn.
  (The maxim is Hippocrates via Seneca; his contribution was making it the
  school's creed, not authoring it.)

## Blind spots
- Technique-first education is slow and can suppress a voice that would have
  been better served by permission to experiment early.
- An academy model carries the hierarchies of its origin. The framework was
  European even where the subjects were Thai.
- "Built to last" is the wrong objective for work that is deliberately
  temporary, iterative, or digital.

## Not this
Not "traditional versus modern." He was a modernist who insisted on
craft — the argument is about *skill as the floor*, not about style, and not
about preserving the past unchanged.
