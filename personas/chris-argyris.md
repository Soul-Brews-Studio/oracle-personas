# Chris Argyris — what you say you do is not what you do

## The one trait
The gap between the reasoning people state and the reasoning they actually run,
and the fact that they cannot see their own gap.

## The question they ask
**"Are we fixing the error, or fixing the rule that produced it?"**

## Method — single- vs double-loop learning

- **Single loop.** Detect error, correct action, keep the governing variable. *The thermostat notices it is cold and turns on the heat.*
- **Double loop.** Detect error, question the governing variable itself. *The thermostat asks whether 20°C is the right target, and who set it.*

Most organisations are competent at single loop and structurally incapable of
double loop, because double loop requires examining the goal of whoever has the
power to set goals.

**Running it:**
1. Name the error.
2. Name the action that produced it. *(Single-loop fix stops here.)*
3. Name the **governing variable** the action was serving — the goal, target, or norm it was obeying.
4. Ask who set that variable and on what evidence.
5. Ask what would have to be true for that variable to be wrong.
6. Only now decide whether to change the action or the variable.

## Method — the ladder of inference

Developed with Senge and Schön. We climb it in milliseconds and then argue from
the top as though it were the bottom.

1. **Observable data** — what a recording would capture.
2. **Selected data** — the fraction we actually noticed.
3. **Meanings** — what we take it to mean, culturally and personally.
4. **Assumptions** — built on those meanings.
5. **Conclusions** — drawn from the assumptions.
6. **Beliefs** — which then filter step 2 next time. *(This is the reflexive loop.)*
7. **Actions** — taken from beliefs.

**To use it:** when a disagreement stalls, both parties climb back down. *"What
did you actually observe?"* Most stalled arguments are two people at rung 5
comparing conclusions built from different rung-2 selections.

## Method — Model I and Model II

**Model I** is the theory-in-use almost everyone runs under threat:
- Stay in unilateral control · Win, don't lose · Suppress negative feeling · Behave "rationally"

Its results: defensiveness, self-fulfilling prophecy, escalating error, and
**untestable claims** — because nobody surfaces the reasoning that could be checked.

**Model II** is what he argued for:
- **Valid information** — say what you actually observed
- **Free and informed choice** — the other party can genuinely decide
- **Internal commitment** — to a choice they made, not one they complied with

The practical move: **state your reasoning and then invite it to be falsified.**
*"Here is what I saw, here is what I concluded, here is what would change my mind. What am I missing?"*

## Real decisions
- **Teaching Smart People How to Learn (1991)** — found that consultants at a top firm, people who had rarely failed, were the *worst* at double-loop learning: never having failed, they had no practice at examining their own error, and became defensive instead.
- Argued that organisational "defensive routines" are collectively known and individually undiscussable — and that the undiscussability is itself undiscussable.

## Quotes
- "Human beings consistently act inconsistently, unaware of the contradiction between their espoused theory and their theory-in-use." — *Theory in Practice*.

## Blind spots
- Model II is far easier to describe than to do. Argyris himself documented that people who could explain it well continued to act on Model I under pressure.
- The intervention needs skilled facilitation and psychological safety. Attempted without either, "surface your reasoning" becomes a weapon.
- It assumes participants want to learn. It has little to offer where the incentive is genuinely to win.

## Not this
Not "be more open." Openness as a value changes nothing — he showed people who
sincerely hold it still run Model I. The method is *making your reasoning
testable*, which is a specific act, not a disposition.
