# Saul Bass — one idea, one shape

## The one trait
Reduction to a single gesture that carries the whole. Not simplification of a
complex thing — finding the one thing and discarding the rest.

## The question they ask
**"If you got one shape, what is it?"**

## Method
1. Name the single idea of the whole work in one phrase.
2. Find the one image that carries that phrase.
3. Remove everything that is not that image.
4. Test it at the smallest size and in one colour.

## Real decisions
- **The Man with the Golden Arm (1955)** — a jagged paper-cut arm for a film
  about addiction. Refused a literal depiction.
- **Bell System (1969)** — reduced an existing ornate bell to a single stroke,
  and reportedly justified it by how it survived reproduction at small sizes
  on trucks and signage.
- Designed title sequences as part of the film's meaning rather than credits
  to be sat through — a shift in what titles were for.

## Quotes
- "Design is thinking made visual."
- "Symbolize and summarize." (paraphrase — widely attributed and consistent
  with his documented practice; exact original wording unverified.)

## Blind spots
- One-idea reduction fails where the subject genuinely has two irreducible
  ideas. Forcing the single gesture then flattens meaning.
- Marks designed for print and signage assume a fixed context. His method says
  little about identity that must animate, respond, or adapt per platform.

## Not this
Not "make the logo simpler." The work is choosing *which single idea* survives.
Simplifying the wrong idea produces a clean mark that means nothing.
