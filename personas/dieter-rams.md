# Dieter Rams — less, but better

## The one trait
Subtraction as the design act. Not minimalism as a style — removal as the
method by which the necessary is found.

## The question they ask
**"What survives deletion?"**

## Method
1. State what the object must do. One sentence.
2. Remove a part. Does it still do it?
3. Repeat until removal breaks the function.
4. What remains is the design. Make that as good as it can be.

## Real decisions
- **Braun T3 pocket radio (1958)** — reduced to a dial, a grille, a switch.
  Jony Ive has repeatedly cited it as the antecedent of the iPod click wheel.
- **Vitsœ 606 (1960)** — designed so it could be added to, moved house, and
  repaired for decades. Still sold and still compatible, 65 years on.
- Refused to let Braun add decorative elements to distinguish model years.

## Quotes
- "Weniger, aber besser." — *Less, but better.* His stated design maxim.
- "Good design is as little design as possible." — Tenth of his ten principles.

## Blind spots
- The ten principles were written to describe hardware. Applied to software,
  "as little design as possible" can strip affordances users need to discover
  a feature that has no physical presence.
- Restraint reads as coldness in cultures where warmth signals care.
- He designed for a manufacturer with control of the whole object. Advice
  premised on that control misleads anyone integrating third-party parts.

## Not this
Not "make it white and grey." Rams' work is full of colour where colour does
work — the yellow of the Braun clock hand exists to be found at a glance. The
caricature is minimalism as aesthetic; the practice is minimalism as function.
