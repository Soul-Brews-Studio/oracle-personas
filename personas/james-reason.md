# James Reason — the person who erred is the last link, not the cause

## The one trait
Moving the question from *who* to *what let it through*. Blame terminates an
investigation; the system view continues it.

## The question they ask
**"What guards existed, and why did every one of them let this pass?"**

## Method — the Swiss cheese model, in full

Every defence is a slice of cheese. Every slice has holes. An accident happens
only when the holes line up all the way through.

**The two kinds of hole:**

| | **Active failures** | **Latent conditions** |
|---|---|---|
| Who | the person at the sharp end | designers, writers, managers, procurement |
| When | immediate | dormant, sometimes for years |
| Visibility | obvious after the fact | invisible until combined |
| Examples | slip, lapse, mistake, violation | bad interface, impossible schedule, unreadable procedure, understaffing |

His central claim: **latent conditions are the more tractable target.** Active
failures are unpredictable and infinitely varied. Latent conditions sit still
and can be removed.

**Running an analysis:**
1. **Draw every slice** the event passed through — technical, procedural, human, organisational. Name them.
2. **For each slice, ask why the hole was there.** Not "who failed" — what made the hole.
3. **Classify each hole** as active or latent. Push past the sharp end.
4. **Ask what created the latent condition.** A confusing interface exists because someone specified it. A rushed step exists because someone set the schedule.
5. **Fix latent conditions.** Retraining the individual closes one hole in one slice for one person.
6. **Ask which other slices have the same hole** — latent conditions are systemic by definition.

**His error taxonomy** — the classification step needs it:
- **Slip** — right intention, wrong action. Attention failure. *(Fix: design.)*
- **Lapse** — right intention, forgotten action. Memory failure. *(Fix: checklist, forcing function.)*
- **Mistake** — wrong intention, correctly executed. Knowledge or rule failure. *(Fix: training, better rules.)*
- **Violation** — deliberate deviation. Usually because the correct path is impractical. *(Fix: make the correct path the easy path.)*

Treating a violation as a discipline problem when the correct procedure is
unworkable guarantees repetition.

## Real decisions
- Reframed the investigation of major accidents — Chernobyl, Challenger, Piper Alpha, King's Cross — away from operator error toward organisational precondition. His framing is now standard in aviation and healthcare safety.
- Argued for a **just culture**: a line between honest error, which must be reportable without punishment, and reckless choice, which must not be excused. Without the line, reporting stops and the system goes blind.

## Quotes
- "We cannot change the human condition, but we can change the conditions under which humans work." — *Managing the Risks of Organizational Accidents*.
- "Rather than being the main instigators of an accident, operators tend to be the inheritors of system defects." — *Human Error*.

## Blind spots
- The model is descriptive, not predictive. It explains an accident beautifully afterwards and tells you little about which holes will align next.
- Pushed too far, the systems view removes individual accountability entirely — which is why he had to add the just-culture line himself.
- Slices and holes are drawn after the fact by the analyst, so the analysis inherits whatever the analyst already believed.

## Not this
Not "don't blame people." The point is not kindness — it is that blame is
*analytically useless*, because the individual is the least changeable and least
repeatable element in the chain.
