# Meredith Belbin — a team of the best people is not the best team

## The one trait
Team composition as a *behavioural* problem, not a talent problem. What matters
is the spread of contributions, not the quality of the individuals.

## The question they ask
**"Which contribution is missing from this team — and who is doing two jobs to cover it?"**

## Method — the nine team roles

Grouped by orientation. Every role has a defined **allowable weakness**: the
cost that comes with the contribution and must be tolerated, not corrected.

**Thinking roles**

| Role | Contributes | Allowable weakness |
|---|---|---|
| **Plant** | Creative, solves hard problems unconventionally | Ignores detail; poor communicator |
| **Monitor Evaluator** | Sober, sees all options, judges accurately | Lacks drive; can be over-critical |
| **Specialist** | Deep single-domain expertise | Contributes on a narrow front only |

**Action roles**

| Role | Contributes | Allowable weakness |
|---|---|---|
| **Shaper** | Drive and courage to overcome obstacles | Prone to provocation; offends feelings |
| **Implementer** | Turns ideas into actions, reliable | Somewhat inflexible; slow to new options |
| **Completer Finisher** | Polishes, finds errors, delivers on time | Anxious; reluctant to delegate |

**People roles**

| Role | Contributes | Allowable weakness |
|---|---|---|
| **Coordinator** | Clarifies goals, delegates well, chairs | Can be seen as manipulative; offloads work |
| **Teamworker** | Diplomatic, listens, averts friction | Indecisive in crunch situations |
| **Resource Investigator** | Explores outside, develops contacts | Over-optimistic; loses interest after initial enthusiasm |

**Running it:**
1. Identify the roles present, from **observed behaviour**, not self-image.
2. Find the gaps. A team with no Completer Finisher ships late and buggy; with no Monitor Evaluator it commits to the first plausible plan.
3. Find the duplicates. Two Shapers is conflict; three Plants is ideas nobody implements.
4. Fill gaps by **reassignment before hiring** — most people can run a secondary role.
5. Accept the weaknesses. Correcting a Plant's inattention to detail removes the Plant.

**Match to task stage:** Plants and Resource Investigators early, Monitor
Evaluator at the decision, Implementers and Completer Finishers late. A team
that never changes shape performs well in only one phase.

## Real decisions
- The **Apollo syndrome**: at Henley he assembled teams entirely of the highest-scoring analytical individuals. They consistently *underperformed* — spending time in abortive debate, converging on nothing, and neglecting execution. This is the finding that made the work matter.
- Argued the ninth role, Specialist, into a model that originally had eight, after fieldwork showed deep expertise did not fit the existing categories.

## Quotes
- "Nobody's perfect, but a team can be." — his best-known formulation.

## Blind spots
- The self-perception inventory has weak psychometric support; independent studies have questioned its reliability and factor structure. Treat the roles as a *vocabulary for observation*, not a valid test.
- Roles get used as identity — "I'm a Plant" — which is exactly the fixed-trait reading the fieldwork does not support. They are behaviours in a context.
- Derived from 1970s British management games. Generalisation to distributed, cross-cultural, or asynchronous teams is assumed rather than demonstrated.

## Not this
Not a personality test. Not something to sort people with permanently. The unit
of analysis is **the team's coverage**, and the same person occupies different
roles in different teams.
