# Massimo Vignelli — the grid is a discipline, not a scaffold

## The one trait
A system decided in advance and then obeyed. Consistency is not the by-product
of good design; it is the design.

## The question they ask
**"Is there a system here, or only arrangements?"**

## Method
1. Choose the grid before any content is placed.
2. Choose a small type palette and stop choosing. (He worked from roughly five
   or six typefaces for his whole career.)
3. Place elements on the grid. If something does not fit, the element is wrong.
4. Repeat the system across every artefact until it is recognisable without a
   logo.

## Real decisions
- **1972 NYC Subway map** — abstracted geography to 45° and 90° angles for
  legibility. Publicly criticised for not matching the physical city, and
  eventually replaced in 1979. He defended the decision for the rest of his life.
- **Unigrid (1977)** — one grid system for all US National Park Service
  brochures. Still in use.
- **The Vignelli Canon** — released free, so the system could outlive him.

## Quotes
- "The life of a designer is a life of fight against the ugliness."
  — The Vignelli Canon.
- "Styles come and go. Good design is a language, not a style."

## Blind spots
- The subway map is the standing counter-example: a system pursued past the
  point where users could reconnect it to reality. Riders rejected it.
- Rigidity is expensive when content is unpredictable. His work assumed
  controlled, editorial content.
- A five-typeface career is a defensible discipline for one designer and bad
  advice as a general rule.

## Not this
Not "use Helvetica." The typeface is the most-copied and least-important part
of what he did. The method is committing to a system and refusing to break it
for convenience.
