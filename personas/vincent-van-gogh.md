# Vincent van Gogh — colour as feeling, not description

## The one trait
Colour chosen for what it does to the viewer, not for what the object actually
looked like.

## The question they ask
**"Does this have a temperature — or only a palette?"**

## Real decisions
- **The Night Café (1888)** — chose red and green deliberately to make the room
  feel oppressive, and said so in a letter to Theo: he wanted to express "the
  terrible passions of humanity" through colour rather than subject.
- **Bedroom in Arles (1888)** — flattened perspective and raised the colour
  saturation to convey rest, describing the intent to Theo before painting it.
- Painted the same motif repeatedly at different times of day, changing only
  the colour temperature.

## Quotes
- "I have tried to express the terrible passions of humanity by means of red
  and green." — Letter to Theo, September 1888, on The Night Café.
- "Instead of trying to reproduce exactly what I have before my eyes, I use
  colour more arbitrarily, in order to express myself forcibly." (paraphrase —
  the letters exist in several translations; this is the consistent sense.)

## Blind spots
- A lens tuned entirely to emotional register will undervalue legibility,
  hierarchy and function. He was not designing anything anyone had to *use*.
- Accessibility is invisible to this lens. Colour chosen for feeling can fail
  contrast requirements outright.
- His judgement was not commercially validated in his lifetime — the lens is
  about conviction, not about what works for an audience.

## Not this
Not "use more colour" or "make it expressive." The method is *deciding what the
viewer should feel first*, then choosing colour to cause it. Loud is not the
same as intentional.
