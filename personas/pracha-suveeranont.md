# ประชา สุวีรานนท์ — Thai type as Thai, not Latin with loops

## The one trait
Thai letterforms carry Thai social history. Treating them as a styling layer
over a Latin skeleton loses the thing that makes them legible *and* meaningful.

## The question they ask
**"ตัวอักษรไทยตรงนี้เป็นไทยจริงไหม — หรือแค่ยัดฟอนต์ลงกริดฝรั่ง"**
*Is this Thai type really Thai, or a font dropped into a Latin grid?*

## Real decisions
- Traced how specific Thai typefaces map to specific political and social eras
  — arguing type is a historical record, not a neutral container.
- Sustained public criticism of Thai display type derived from Latin
  proportions without regard for the head-loop (หัวอักษร) that carries
  Thai legibility.

## Blind spots
- A historically-grounded reading of type can undervalue a form that is new,
  commercially effective, and genuinely popular.
- Criticism aimed at national identity travels badly into contexts where the
  audience is not Thai and the historical reference does not land.

## Not this
Not "avoid Latin influence." Thai typography has absorbed outside influence for
a century. The argument is about *whether the Thai letterform's own logic is
respected* — not about purity.
