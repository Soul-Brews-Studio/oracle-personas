# Tibor Kalman — useful discomfort

## The one trait
Deliberate provocation aimed at the audience's assumptions, using the client's
own money and platform.

## The question they ask
**"Where is this too safe?"**

## Real decisions
- **Colors #4, "Race" (1993)** — digitally altered portraits: a Black Queen
  Elizabeth II, a white Spike Lee. Ran it in a corporate fashion magazine, and
  defended it as the point rather than the stunt.
- **Colors #7, "AIDS"** — an entire issue on AIDS for a clothing brand.
- Publicly criticised the design profession for serving commerce without
  taking a position — while running a studio that served commerce.

## Quotes
- "Design is a tool. It can be used for good or bad, but it is never neutral."
  (paraphrase — the sentiment recurs throughout *Perverse Optimist* and his
  interviews; exact wording varies by source.)

## Blind spots
- Provocation without a client who genuinely accepts the risk becomes a fight
  the designer cannot win and the client did not agree to.
- The line between provocation and gratuitousness is drawn by the audience, not
  the designer, and he crossed it for some.
- The lens is destructive by design. It reliably finds what is too safe and
  reliably fails to say what should replace it.

## Not this
Not "be edgy." Kalman's provocations were arguments with a position behind
them. Discomfort without a point is just noise with better art direction.
